\section{The certificates}\label{app:cert114}

This appendix proves
\Cref{prop:family2,prop:family44,prop:family52a,prop:family52b,prop:family51a,prop:family51b}.
Each proof is a finite computation in interval arithmetic: every number below is an exact
rational ball, and every operation returns a ball that contains the exact
result. We used Arb \cite{Joh17} at a working precision of $1000$ bits. The
programs and the data are archived with the repository of this paper. We
describe the computation for \Cref{prop:family2} in detail; the others
have the same structure and are summarized at the end.

\subsection*{The Krawczyk test}
Write $x\in\CC^{24}$ for the coordinates $(f,h,g,a,b,c)$ of
\Cref{ssec:twofamilies}, in this order, and $E(x,\kappa)\in\CC^{24}$ for
the vector of coefficients $E_{0},\dots,E_{23}$. Fix
$\kappa_{0}=(-17+3i)/10$ and a point $\tilde x\in\CC^{24}$ whose real and
imaginary parts are decimal numbers with $120$ significant digits, and let
$X\subseteq\CC^{24}$ be the box of points whose real and imaginary parts
differ from those of $\tilde x$ by at most $10^{-95}$. Let $Y$ be a complex
matrix close to $J(\tilde x,\kappa_{0})^{-1}$ (with exact rational entries),
and let
\[
  K(X)=\tilde x-Y\,E(\tilde x,\kappa_{0})+\bigl(I-Y\,J(X,\kappa_{0})\bigr)
  (X-\tilde x),
\]
evaluated in interval arithmetic, where $J(X,\kappa_{0})$ is an interval
matrix containing the Jacobian at every point of $X$. Regarding $\CC^{24}$
as $\RR^{48}$, the holomorphic map $E(\cdot,\kappa_{0})$ becomes a smooth
map of $\RR^{48}$, the complex interval computations enclose the
corresponding real ones, and Krawczyk's theorem applies: if $K(X)$ lies in
the interior of $X$, then $E(\cdot,\kappa_{0})$ has exactly one zero $x^{*}$
in $X$, and every matrix in $J(X,\kappa_{0})$, in particular
$J(x^{*},\kappa_{0})$, is invertible \cite{Kra69,Moo77}, \cite[\S5.1]{Neu90}.

The computation gives $K(X)$ inside the interior of $X$, with every
coordinate of $K(X)$ of radius below $10^{-180}$. Let $p^{*}=(x^{*},
\kappa_{0})$. This proves \Cref{prop:family2}(i).

\subsection*{Nondegeneracy}
On the box $X$, the balls enclosing $a$, $b$, $c$ and $1+a+b$, and the
resultants of the six pairs among $F,H,G,K$, computed as determinants of
Sylvester matrices, do not contain $0$. Their absolute values exceed
$0.46$, $0.50$, $0.070$, $1.69$, and, for the resultants of $(F,G)$, $(F,H)$,
$(F,K)$, $(K,G)$, $(H,G)$ and $(H,K)$, $9.7\cdot10^{-6}$, $0.47$, $0.88$,
$0.14$, $0.19$ and $1.0$. This proves \Cref{prop:family2}(ii).

\subsection*{The residue}
The tangent vector $v^{*}$ has $\kappa$-component $1$, and its other
components are $\dot x=-J^{-1}\,\partial E/\partial\kappa$, which we enclose
by solving the linear system over the balls of $X$. With the derivatives of
$F,H,G$ and of $a,b,c$ given by $\dot x$, and $\dot K=-s(s-1)$, we enclose
the coefficients of the polynomials $\mathcal D_{v^{*}}$ and $\mathcal M$ of
\Cref{ssec:twofamilies}, reduce both modulo $F^{5}$, and solve the
$35\times35$ linear system for the coefficients of $R_{v^{*}}$; Arb's
solver certifies that the system is invertible. The coefficient of $s^{34}$
is enclosed in
\[
  r_{34}\in[75.2385455180\pm1.1\cdot10^{-11}]+[-19.2657017116\pm
  3.1\cdot10^{-11}]\,i,
\]
which does not contain $0$; its absolute value exceeds $77.66$. By the
discussion after \Cref{cor:family2}, $I_{03}(p^{*},v^{*})=r_{34}\ne0$,
which proves \Cref{prop:family2}(iii).

\subsection*{Independent checks}
The point $\tilde x$ was found by Newton's method from a homotopy, and the
same computation repeated at $600$ bits gives the same conclusions. The
equality $B_{114}=S_{114}+\ZZ[\beta]$ of \Cref{cor:B114} is also checked
without \Cref{thm:aoki}: the integer matrix whose rows are the classes of
the $113$ pairs, the $98$ standard multisets of level $114$, the element
$(57)$ and $[\beta]$ has rank $95$ and is saturated, as its Smith normal form
shows, while the lattice of $x=\sum_{y}c_{y}(y)\in R_{114}$ with
$\sum_{y}c_{y}\overline{ty}=57\sum_{y}c_{y}$ for every unit $t$ has rank
$114-1-18=95$; since $(57)$ has odd length and $2\,(57)$ lies in $D_{114}$,
this gives $B_{114}=S_{114}+\ZZ[\beta]$. The same computation at level $57$
gives $[B_{57}:S_{57}]=2$.

\subsection*{The family of degree \texorpdfstring{$44$}{44}}
For \Cref{prop:family44} the unknowns are $x=(p,q,r_{1},c_{0},c_{1},c_{3})\in
\CC^{12}$ and the equations are $E_{0},\dots,E_{11}$ of
\Cref{ssec:degree44}. We fix $\kappa_{0}=(4-3i)/10$, a point $\tilde x$ with
$120$ significant digits, and the box $X$ of radius $10^{-95}$ around it.
The Krawczyk image $K(X)$ lies in the interior of $X$, with coordinates of
radius below $10^{-185}$; this gives the zero $x^{*}$, the point
$p^{\dagger}=(x^{*},\kappa_{0})$ and part (i). On $X$ the balls enclosing
$c_{0}$, $c_{1}$, $c_{3}$, $p_{0}$, $q_{0}$ and the resultants of $(P,Q)$,
$(P,S)$ and $(Q,S)$ do not contain $0$; their absolute values exceed
$0.52$, $0.80$, $0.30$, $1.57$, $0.89$, $1.05$, $4.42$ and $2.66$, and
$|\kappa_{0}|=1/2$. This is part (ii).

For part (iii) we enclose $\dot x=-J'^{-1}\partial E/\partial\kappa$ on $X$,
form the polynomial $\mathcal N_{v^{\dagger}}$ of \Cref{ssec:degree44} and
the denominator $c_{0}c_{1}c_{3}s^{3}P^{2}Q^{3}S$, and expand their quotient
at $s=\infty$ by division of power series in $s^{-1}$, whose leading
coefficient $c_{0}c_{1}c_{3}$ does not vanish. The coefficient of $s^{-1}$ is
enclosed in a disc of radius $3\cdot10^{-71}$ around
\[
  I_{02}(p^{\dagger},v^{\dagger})\approx-0.177444420578390+0.065416149270899\,i,
\]
whose absolute value exceeds $0.189$. By the discussion after
\Cref{cor:family44} this proves (iii). As a check on the regularity used
there, every coefficient of the remainder of $\mathcal N_{v^{\dagger}}$
modulo $P^{2}S$ is enclosed in a ball of numbers of absolute value below
$10^{-70}$. The same
computation at $600$ bits gives the same conclusions, and the lattice
computation described above, at level $44$ with the $43$ pairs, the $24$
standard multisets, the element $(22)$ and $[\beta]$, gives rank $33$, the
rank of the lattice of balanced classes, and a saturated matrix; so
$B_{44}=S_{44}+\ZZ[\beta]$ and $[B_{44}:S_{44}]=2$ without \Cref{thm:aoki}.

\subsection*{The families of degree \texorpdfstring{$52$}{52}}
For \Cref{prop:family52a} the unknowns are $x=(g,f,c_{0},c_{1},c_{3},c_{4})
\in\CC^{15}$ and the equations are $E_{0},\dots,E_{14}$ of
\Cref{ssec:degree52}. We fix $\kappa_{0}=-(3+2i)/10$, a point $\tilde x$
with $120$ significant digits, and the box $X$ of radius $10^{-95}$ around
it. The Krawczyk image $K(X)$ lies in the interior of $X$, with coordinates
of radius below $10^{-184}$; this gives the zero $x^{*}$, the point
$p^{A}=(x^{*},\kappa_{0})$ and part (i). On $X$ the balls enclosing $c_{0}$,
$c_{1}$, $c_{3}$, $c_{4}$, $g_{0}$, $f_{0}$ and the resultants of $(G,F)$,
$(G,H)$ and $(F,H)$ do not contain $0$; their absolute values exceed $3.16$,
$3.04$, $0.139$, $1.24$, $0.69$, $0.121$, $56.4$, $5.05$ and $0.644$. This
is part (ii).

For part (iii) we enclose $\dot x=-J_{A}^{-1}\partial E/\partial\kappa$ on
$X$, form the polynomial $\mathcal N_{v^{A}}$ and the denominator
$c_{0}c_{1}c_{3}c_{4}s^{2}F^{3}GH^{3}$ of \Cref{ssec:degree52}, and expand
their quotient at $s=\infty$ by division of power series in $s^{-1}$, whose
leading coefficient $c_{0}c_{1}c_{3}c_{4}$ does not vanish. The coefficient
of $s^{-1}$ is enclosed in a disc of radius $5\cdot10^{-68}$ around
\[
  I_{02}(p^{A},v^{A})\approx-5.38867785237614+19.2154179849628\,i,
\]
whose absolute value exceeds $19.95$.

For \Cref{prop:family52b} the unknowns are $x=(q_{0},q_{1},c_{0},c_{1},c_{3},
c_{4})\in\CC^{6}$, and we fix $\kappa_{0}=(3+6i)/10$. The Krawczyk image of
the box of radius $10^{-95}$ around a point with $120$ significant digits
lies in its interior, with coordinates of radius below $10^{-187}$. On the
box, $c_{0}$, $c_{1}$, $c_{3}$, $c_{4}$, and the resultants of $(s,L)$,
$(s,Q)$, $(s-t,L)$, $(s-t,Q)$ and $(L,Q)$, which are $\kappa_{0}$, $q_{0}$,
$1+\kappa_{0}$, $1+q_{1}+q_{0}$ and $\kappa_{0}^{2}-q_{1}\kappa_{0}+q_{0}$,
have absolute values above $0.58$, $0.29$, $1.31$, $0.61$, $0.67$, $1.81$,
$1.43$, $1.35$ and $1.50$. The coefficient of $s^{-1}$ in the expansion of
$\mathcal N_{v^{B}}/(c_{0}c_{1}c_{3}c_{4}s^{3}(s-1)^{3}L^{3}Q)$ is enclosed in
a disc of radius $4\cdot10^{-87}$ around
\[
  I_{12}(p^{B},v^{B})\approx-5.53944549467493-5.38246966308485\,i,
\]
whose absolute value exceeds $7.72$. Both computations give the same
conclusions at $600$ bits. The lattice computation, at level $52$ with the
$51$ pairs, the $28$ standard multisets, the element $(26)$ and $[\beta]$,
gives rank $39$, the rank of the lattice of balanced classes, and a saturated
matrix; so $B_{52}=S_{52}+\ZZ[\beta]$ and $[B_{52}:S_{52}]=2$ without
\Cref{thm:aoki}.

\subsection*{The families of degree \texorpdfstring{$102$}{102}}
For \Cref{prop:family51a} the unknowns are the coordinates other than
$\kappa$, $x=(q_{0},q_{1},f_{1},c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},c_{4})\in\CC^{7}$, and the
equations are $E_{0},\dots,E_{6}$ of \Cref{ssec:degree51}. We fix $\kappa_{0}=-(637+53i)/500$, a point $\tilde x$
with $320$ significant digits, and the box $X$ of radius $10^{-95}$ around
it. The Krawczyk image $K(X)$ lies in the interior of $X$, with coordinates
of radius below $10^{-187}$; this gives the zero $x^{*}$, the point
$p_{6}=(x^{*},\kappa_{0})$ and part (i). On $X$ the balls enclosing $c_{1}$,
$c_{2}$, $c_{3}$, $c_{4}$, $q_{0}$ and the resultant of $(Q,F)$ do not
contain $0$; their absolute values exceed $1.02$, $1.04$, $1.73$, $1.35$,
$0.344$ and $1.32$. This is part (ii). For part (iii) we enclose $\dot x=
-J_{6}^{-1}\partial E/\partial\kappa$ on $X$, form $\mathcal N_{v_{6}}$ and
the denominator $c_{1}c_{2}c_{3}c_{4}s^{2}Q^{2}F^{3}$ of
\Cref{ssec:degree51}, and expand their quotient at $s=\infty$. The
coefficient of $s^{-1}$ is enclosed in a disc of radius $5\cdot10^{-90}$
around
\[
  I_{04}(p_{6},v_{6})\approx1.13299117817861+1.06868517120741\,i,
\]
whose absolute value exceeds $1.55$.

For \Cref{prop:family51b} the unknowns are $x=(q_{1},f,g,c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},
c_{4})\in\CC^{19}$, and we fix $\kappa_{0}=-(147+117i)/500$. The Krawczyk
image of the box of radius $10^{-95}$ around a point with $320$ significant
digits lies in its interior, with coordinates of radius below $10^{-183}$.
On the box, $c_{1}$, $c_{2}$, $c_{3}$, $c_{4}$, and the resultants of
$(Q,F)$, $(Q,s)$, $(Q,G)$, $(F,s)$ and $(F,G)$, the second and fourth of
which are $\kappa_{0}$ and $f_{0}$ up to sign, have absolute values above
$1.61$, $2.13$, $0.914$, $1.62$, $0.548$, $0.375$, $0.716$, $0.685$ and
$0.078$; the resultant of $(s,G)$ is $1$. The coefficient of $s^{-1}$ in the
expansion of $\mathcal N_{v_{18}}/(c_{1}c_{2}c_{3}c_{4}s^{4}Q^{5}FG^{5})$ is
enclosed in a disc of radius $9\cdot10^{-77}$ around
\[
  I_{23}(p_{18},v_{18})\approx-2.46442837913866-3.88281035492264\,i,
\]
whose absolute value exceeds $4.59$. Both computations give the same
conclusions at $600$ bits. The lattice computation, at level $102$ with the
$101$ pairs, the $88$ standard multisets, the element $(51)$ and $[\beta]$,
gives rank $85$, the rank of the lattice of balanced classes, and a saturated
matrix; so $B_{102}=S_{102}+\ZZ[\beta]$ and $[B_{102}:S_{102}]=2$ without
\Cref{thm:aoki}.
